# Art production: the remaster

The decision is taken: the game moves from the generated placeholder art to the approved concept
direction. This file is the complete scope and the build order. It is measured from the shipped
files, not estimated.

Companion boards: the **Art bible** band on the design canvas carries a reference image beside every
group below. Where this file says "reference", that image exists. Where it says "no reference", it
does not, and a design pass has to come first.

Source of the direction: `reference/mocks/gameplay-concepts.png`, the approved concept board.
Binding for anything that is UI rather than world art: `docs/UI_FOUNDATIONS.md`.

---

## 1. The one decision everything waits on: 16px or 32px

**Measured.** Red is **24px** tall in the game. The same character in the approved art is **60px**.
The concept direction is drawn at **2.5× the game's current scale**. That is the whole reason the
placeholder reads as placeholder: a 16×16 grass tile has 256 pixels to work with, and the reference
is using roughly six times that area for the same square of ground.

**Proposal: move to 32px tiles.**

|               | now                     | proposed                  |
| ------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Tile          | 16×16                   | 32×32                     |
| Red           | 16×24                   | 32×48                     |
| NPCs          | 16×24                   | 32×48                     |
| Portraits     | 32×32                   | 64×64                     |
| Items         | 32×32                   | 64×64                     |
| World         | 960×672 px              | 1920×1344 px              |
| Camera        | 414×239 px, upscaled 3× | 828×478 px, upscaled 1.5× |
| Field of view | 26×15 tiles             | 26×15 tiles, unchanged    |

32px is not quite the 40px that an exactly faithful 2.5× would give. It is the right call anyway:
it doubles linear detail, it is a conventional size that any pixel artist will quote against, and it
keeps the upscale factor a clean 1.5×.

**What does not change.** The map is 60×42 tiles and stays 60×42 tiles. Layout, collision, triggers
and object positions are all generated by `tools/graybox/src/forest-map.ts` from a `TILE` constant,
so the level survives the change untouched. The field of view stays the same, so no encounter, no
trigger and no walk distance needs retuning.

**What does change, in code.**

- `apps/game/src/world/logic.ts` — `export const TILE = 16` becomes `32`.
- `tools/graybox/src/forest-map.ts` — the same constant, then regenerate.
- The camera size and the integer upscale in `apps/game/src/world/scenes.ts`.
- `apps/game/src/assets/manifest.json` — every `frameWidth` / `frameHeight`.
- `apps/game/test/map.test.ts` and the e2e walk budgets assume pixel distances; they double.

**What cannot be salvaged.** None of the 106 existing frames. Upscaled 16px art looks exactly like
upscaled 16px art. Every frame below is drawn again.

## 3. Terrain, 54 tiles

The largest single block, and the one with the most reference. Nothing else can be judged until the
ground is right, so this goes first.

**Ground, 14 tiles.** `grass-0..3`, `path`, eight path edges and corners, `paving`.
The reference uses three greens plus a darker clump and a lighter blade highlight. Variation arrives
in patches several tiles across, never per tile: the value-noise field in `forest-map.ts` already
does this and only needs a wider palette. Path edges in the reference are irregular, two pixels of
lighter sand and then a dark grass lip. No straight edge anywhere on a path tile.

**Water, 7 tiles.** `water`, `water-2`, four shores, `waterfall`.
A mid blue, a lighter band, and a white foam line where water meets bank. The bank is rock where it
is steep and sand only where the path crosses. The waterfall needs three frames, which the current
tileset does not have: budget **9 tiles**, not 7.

**Trees, 6 tiles → 12.** Today a 2×2 canopy over two trunk halves. The reference tree is a 3-wide
canopy over a single trunk, with a cast shadow on the ground beneath it. Budget a 3×3 canopy, two
trunk tiles and a shadow tile.

**Ground cover, 6 tiles.** Three flowers, bush, rock, toadstool ring.
The reference never places a flower alone on grass; flowers come in threes, growing out of a bush.
Draw the bush first and the flowers as variants of it.

**Built, 13 tiles → 20.** Fence, bridge, and the cottage.
The fence in the reference is a picket fence with a post every two tiles and two rails, which needs
three tiles rather than today's two. The cottage needs scalloped roof tiles, a stone surround to the
door, window boxes and ivy: budget 14 for the building, 3 for the fence, 3 for the bridge.

**Props, 8 tiles → 16.** Stall, lantern post, fountain, well, signpost.
Every one of these is a single 16px tile today and is a 2×2 object in the reference. The stall alone
is awning, counter, goods and a hanging sign.

**Terrain total: 54 → about 76 tiles.** The tileset grows from 192×80 to roughly 320×320.

## 4. Red, 16 frames

16×24 → **32×48**. Four directions × (idle + 3 walk).

The reference Red: a hood with a visible brim, a face with two eye pixels and a blush, a cloak that
falls to the knee, blue trousers, and a basket carried in the off hand. The basket is the detail
that makes her read as on an errand rather than just walking.

At 32×48 there is room for two things the current sprite cannot do. **Recommend 20 frames**: four
directions × (2 idle + 3 walk), so she breathes when she stands still and the basket swings when she
walks. She is on screen for the entire game; this is the single most valuable frame budget increase
in the whole list.

## 5. The cast

**Rabbit** and **Owl** — reference exists on the board. 2 frames → 4 (two idle, two talking).

**Lantern Keeper** — **no reference. The concept board never drew this character.** A design pass is
needed before anything can be drawn. `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` suggests a hedgehog or a badger.
Recommendation: a badger in a hooded coat carrying the lantern on a short pole, so the silhouette
reads at 32px and the lantern, not the face, is the thing you notice. The lantern is the skill.

**Wolf** — the only reference is the silhouette in the lit cottage window. The wolf needs a full
design: as Grandma in bonnet and shawl, plus the reveal. The silhouette in the window is doing a lot
of work in the reference and should stay a silhouette until the reveal.

**Four bystanders** — Toadstool, Magpie, Snail, Cat. No reference for any of them. They are the
cheapest and least risky work here: nobody inspects them closely and none of them is ever scored.
Do them last.

## 6. Portraits and items

**Portraits**, 5 at 32×32 → 64×64. Red and the Rabbit have reference in the approved deck's dialogue
frames. The Owl can be derived from the board. The Keeper and the Wolf cannot: they wait on section 5.

**Items**, 5 at 32×32 → 64×64. The compass and the lantern have reference on the board, as the stall
goods and the hanging lantern. The key, the letter and the translator have none, and the translator
is the hardest because nothing in the fiction says what it looks like.

**Effects**, 3 sheets. Dust 8×8 → 16×16, interact prompt and paw prints 16×16 → 32×32. No reference.
The paw prints matter more than their size suggests: they are the whole ending, in one detail, on the
path outside the door.

## 6a. The rebrand to Cyera purple

Decided alongside the remaster. Boards: the **Rebrand** band on the canvas.

**The rule.** Every asset was scanned for warm red. Red's cloak is **50 to 64 per cent of each of her
sixteen frames** and 39 per cent of her portrait; nothing else is close. That is why she is findable
in a green forest. So she moves to purple **and everything else that is red moves off red**, or she
stops being the one thing your eye catches.

**The world stays a forest.** Cyera's palette has no green, no brown and no water blue. Brand colour
enters through Red, the wolf, the night, the notice and the UI. Lime appears nowhere in the world: it
is one call to action per screen, per `UI_FOUNDATIONS`.

| Element                         | Today                 | Becomes                                             |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Red's cloak                     | Scarlet               | Deep Purple → Lilac → Haze                          |
| Cottage roof, 4 tiles           | Red                   | Slate with Clay highlights                          |
| Well roof                       | Red                   | Slate                                               |
| Stall awning, 3 tiles           | Red and white stripes | Haze and Offwhite stripes                           |
| Toadstool cap and ring tile     | Red, white spots      | Aubergine, Offwhite spots                           |
| Snail shell                     | Red                   | Clay with a Haze swirl                              |
| flower-red / flower-pink        | Red / pink            | Lilac / Haze                                        |
| The wolf                        | Grey-brown            | Black and Deep Purple                               |
| Lantern flame                   | Warm gold             | Unchanged. It is a light source and it is the skill |
| Wax seal on the letter          | Red                   | Deep Purple                                         |
| Grass, water, wood, stone, sand | Natural               | Unchanged                                           |

**Two cloak options**, both produced by recolouring the real sprite with a luminance-preserving ramp:
**A, Lilac-led** (recommended — easiest to find at a 26-tile camera) and **B, Deep Purple-led** (more
obviously Cyera, weaker against tree shadow).

**The cape mark is not settled.** `CLAUDE.md` rule 5 forbids redrawn logo variants, and at 32×48 the
real mark on her cloak is four grey pixels. The proposal is a 9×9 motif that keeps the mark's radial
form without claiming to be the logo, on the four back-facing frames only. **This needs a decision
from brand**: approve the motif as a deliberately-not-the-logo device, or leave the cape plain. Both
are acceptable; a squashed logo is not.

## 7. The order of work

1. **Settle 32px.** Everything below is blocked on it. One afternoon of code, then regenerate.
2. **Terrain, about 76 tiles.** Largest block, most reference, and nothing can be judged without it.
3. **Red, 20 frames.** On screen for the whole game.
4. **Rabbit and Owl, 8 frames.** Reference exists, so these are straight execution.
5. **Design passes: the Lantern Keeper, then the Wolf.** These are decisions, not drawing. Do them
   while terrain is in flight so they are not on the critical path.
6. **Draw the Keeper and the Wolf**, then the four bystanders.
7. **Portraits, items, effects.**
8. **Regenerate the map at `TILE = 32`**, then re-run `pnpm test`, `pnpm sim` and `pnpm e2e`. The map
   test floods from the spawn and will catch a terrain mistake that makes a goal unreachable.

Two things to decide before the first tile is drawn, because they are cheaper now than later:

- **32px or 40px.** 40px is exactly faithful to the reference. 32px is conventional and gives a clean
  1.5× upscale. This file assumes 32.
- **Who draws the Lantern Keeper and the Wolf.** They are the only two characters with no reference
  at all, and they are the two the ending depends on.
